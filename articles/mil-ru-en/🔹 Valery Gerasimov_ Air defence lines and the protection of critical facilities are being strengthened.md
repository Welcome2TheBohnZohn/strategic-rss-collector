Valery Gerasimov: Air defence lines and the protection of critical facilities are being strengthened
Report
by Chief of the General Staff of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation – First Deputy Minister of Defenсe of the Russian Federation
Against the backdrop of a lack of results on the battlefield, the Kiev regime has opted to launch massive strikes against civilian infrastructure facilities in the Russian heartland. In January 2026, Russian air defence systems intercepted an average of around 120 enemy unmanned aerial vehicles and missiles over Russian regions per day. By September, this figure had increased more than fivefold. In September alone, more than 15,000 aerial targets were shot down, amounting to over 500 per day.
The largest number of aerial threats assets was deployed by the enemy between 19 and 20 September in an attempt to disrupt the elections to the State Duma. Within 24 hours, 1,398 strike unmanned aerial vehicles and eight cruise missiles were shot down over Russian regions. Despite the increasing scale and frequency of attacks against Russian civilian infrastructure facilities, Russian air defence destroys more than 98 per cent of enemy drones every day.
Nevertheless, some enemy drones still manage to reach their targets. Efforts to improve air defence capabilities are being carried out continuously. Air defence lines and the protection of critical facilities are being strengthened, while new and effective means of detecting and destroying unmanned aerial vehicles and cruise missiles are being introduced without delay.
In response to strikes by the Kiev regime against facilities on Russian territory, and in accordance with the General Staff's plan, massive and group strikes have continued against key defence industry facilities and logistics centres as well as energy and transport infrastructure facilities throughout Ukraine that support their operation. The highest-priority targets are facilities involved in the production of missile weapons and unmanned aerial vehicles, as well as their storage and launch sites.
