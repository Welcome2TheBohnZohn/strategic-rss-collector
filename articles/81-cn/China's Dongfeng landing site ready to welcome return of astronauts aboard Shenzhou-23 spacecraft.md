China's Dongfeng landing site ready to welcome return of astronauts aboard Shenzhou-23 spacecraft

JIUQUAN, Nov. 27 (Xinhua) -- The Dongfeng landing site in north China has conducted its final full-system search, rescue and recovery drill ahead of the Shenzhou-23 crewed spacecraft's scheduled return to Earth in the coming days.
The exercise on Thursday was designed to ensure the safe return of the three astronauts on board, said Shi Xundong of the Dongfeng landing site.
The drill simulated key mission procedures, including aerial search and rescue and ground handling, while testing the technical status of equipment such as measurement and communication devices.
It also aimed to further verify the landing site system's organization, command and comprehensive support capabilities for the search, rescue and recovery operations, according to the landing site.
To address the difficulty of search and handling operations at night, the landing site refined its operational plans and improved lighting support measures.
Two Shenzhou-23 astronauts, Zhang Zhiyuan and Li Jiaying, together with the Shenzhou-24 mission's Pakistani astronaut Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, are scheduled to return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft.
All preparations at the Dongfeng landing site have now been completed, and the site is ready to welcome the astronauts back from space, according to the landing site.
