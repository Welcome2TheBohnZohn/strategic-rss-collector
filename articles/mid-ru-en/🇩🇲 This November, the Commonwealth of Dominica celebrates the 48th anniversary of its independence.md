This
November, the Commonwealth of Dominica celebrates the 48th anniversary of its independence.
This small island in the Caribbean Sea became the subject of contention between the European colonial powers —
England and France
— in the 17th and 18th centuries.
In their competion for resources, the two colonial states alternately owned the island, until in 1763, following the Seven Years’ War, it finally passed to
the British Crown
.
Slave labour was widely used
here during the period of colonial rule: slaves from West Africa were heavily trafficked there to work on coffee and sugar cane plantations.
In 1967,
#Dominica
, which had been
under the rule of London for two centuries
, received the status of an internally self-governing
Associated State with the United Kingdom
.
Dominica's independence was declared
on November 3, 1978,
yet the country remains within the British Commonwealth to this day.
