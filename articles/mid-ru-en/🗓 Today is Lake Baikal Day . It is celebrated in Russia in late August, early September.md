# 🗓 Today is Lake Baikal Day . It is celebrated in Russia in late August, early September.

🗓
Today is
Lake Baikal Day
. It is celebrated in Russia in late August, early September.
There is no single date, however, as
the lake is located across several regions of the Russian Federation
— Irkutsk Region, the Republic of Buryatia and Zabaykalsky Krai — each of which sets its own date for the celebration.
Thus,
in Buryatia
, Lake Baikal Day is observed on the first Sunday of August;
in Irkutsk Region
— on the first Sunday of September; and
in Zabaykalsky Krai
— on the second Sunday of September. The different dates make it possible to hold environmental, educational and cultural events over several weeks, ensuring
maximum public attention to Lake Baikal
and the need to preserve its unique ecosystem.
The date was first established in 1999 at the initiative of public organisations and environmentalists. The immediate impetus was
#
Baikal
’s inclusion in 1996 on the
#UNESCO
World Heritage List.
The decision was based on the unique combination of geological features significant for the study of the Earth’s evolution, as well as the richness and diversity of Baikal’s flora and fauna. The lake’s inclusion on the UNESCO list confirmed its
global environmental value
.
💬
From the
greeting
by
Alexander Kozlov
, Minister of Natural Resources and Environment of Russia, on the occasion of Lake Baikal Day:
Preserving Baikal and its ecosystems is
one of the priorities of Russia’s state environmental policy.
To this end, a network of specially protected natural areas has been established, and Russia’s first nature reserve, the Barguzinsky Nature Reserve, was created right here, on the shores of Lake Baikal.

🔹
Lake Baikal is 636 km long, stretching from southwest to northeast
in the shape of a giant crescent.
Its width ranges from 24 to 79 km, while its water surface covers
31,722 km²
(excluding islands). The length of its shoreline
exceeds 2,000 km.
Baikal is the deepest lake on the planet.
Its maximum depth reaches
1,642 metres
, while its average depth is approximately 744 metres.
Lake Baikal contains approximately
23,615 km³ of water.
It is the world’s largest natural reservoir of fresh water, holding
around 20% of the world’s surface freshwater reserves.
Baikal is a true museum of endemic species — species found nowhere else on Earth. The lake is home to
around 2,600 species of animals and plants
, approximately 60–70% of which are endemic, including the beloved Baikal seal —
the world’s only freshwater seal species.
