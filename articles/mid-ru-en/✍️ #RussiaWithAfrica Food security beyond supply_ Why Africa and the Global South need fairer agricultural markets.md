Food security beyond supply: Why Africa and the Global South need fairer agricultural markets
✍️
An article by Russian Ambassador to South Africa and Lesotho
Roman Ambarov,
published in
Independent Online (IOL),
The Public Eye
and
Lesotho Tribune
newspapers.
Key takeaways:
Russia: A reliable partner in ensuring food security
🔴
Russia remains
a reliable agricultural partner for Africa,
supplying
over 18 million tonnes
of grain in 2025 and a further
13 million tonnes
in the first nine months of 2026.
🔴
Russian fertiliser exports to Africa
exceeded 2.2 million tonnes
in 2026, helping support agricultural production across the continent.
🔴
Africa has
enormous agricultural potential.
Investment in irrigation, mechanisation, infrastructure, modern technologies and local processing can help the continent
reduce import dependence and become a major agricultural exporter.
🔴
Russia is ready to share its expertise and technologies
in agricultural machinery, seed production, fertilisers, livestock farming, storage, processing and logistics.
🔴
Russia's approach is based on partnership, not dependency
– combining Africa's agricultural resources and development priorities with Russian technological expertise and production capabilities.
Protecting the routes that feed the world
🔴
With Western backing, Kiev has
intensified attacks on Russian civilian vessels
,
grain terminals and agricultural export infrastructure
in the Black Sea and Sea of Azov. August drone strikes on Novorossiysk grain terminals temporarily disrupted facilities with a combined annual capacity of
15.6 million tonnes.
🔴
Russia is adapting its export logistics. The development of
alternative transport corridors, including Arctic routes
, helps maintain the continuity of agricultural exports despite external pressures.
Who determines the price of food?
🔴
Russia is
a major global grain supplier
. It exported approximately
53 million tonnes of grain
in 2024/25, including
44 million tonnes of wheat
, to more than 100 countries, with grain export potential of at least
60 million tonnes.
🔴
Global agricultural prices are largely shaped by Western financial centres.
CBOT, Euronext and ICE play a major role in price formation, raising questions about the fairness and representativeness of the current system.
🔴
The Global South needs a greater voice in agricultural pricing.
It is in the interests of the Global South to develop more representative agricultural trading infrastructure and strengthen its participation in international price formation.
Russia proposes a BRICS Grain Exchange
The initiative aims to give major grain-producing countries a greater role in setting fair, predictable agricultural prices.
🔴
The initiative is gaining momentum. Supported by the
2024 Kazan Declaration
, the proposal advanced in 2026, with the BRICS New Delhi Declaration reaffirming the need to develop the platform and refine its operational modalities.
🔴
Russia-Africa cooperation
should go beyond supplies
to include technology transfer, investment, local processing and strengthening agricultural sovereignty, ensuring African countries become market-shapers rather than price-takers.
