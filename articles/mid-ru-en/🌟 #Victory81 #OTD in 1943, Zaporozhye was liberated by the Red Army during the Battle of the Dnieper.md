The city of
#Zaporozhye

major industrial and logistics hub
, was occupied by the Nazi invaders in the first months of the Great Patriotic War.
◼️
During two years of
Nazi terror
and
occupation
, more than 40'000 civilians and POWs were killed in Zaporozhye by the enemy troops.
The Nazis sought to hold the city at any cost, as it had become a key stronghold in the lower reaches of the Dnieper. The Germans had effectively turned Zaporozhye into a major bridgehead within the system of fortifications along the '
Eastern Wall
' defence line.
⚔️
In the autumn of 1943, the front line was rapidly approaching the Dnieper. On October 4, the units of the Red Army’s Southwestern Front, commanded by would-be Marshal of the Soviet Union
Rodion Yakovlevich Malinovsky
,
launched an offensive in the Zaporozhye sector
.
Having breached the Germans’ outer defence on October 13, the Soviet forces approached the city.
Fierce combat raged for every street, every residential building.
By the end of the day on October 14, following a night-time assault, the Red Army had overcome enemy resistance and liberated Zaporozhye.
The Nazis were deprived of a strategically important bridgehead on the eastern bank of the Dnieper and lost the ability to launch a counterattack. Unable to stop the Red Army’s advance during its the Zaporozhye peration, the Nazi command ordered the Dnieper Hydroelectric Power Plant (DnieproGES) dam to be blown up.
📄
From the testimony given during an interrogation on October 11, 1947 by
Kurt Hans Steffen, a former driver with the 520th Engineer Regiment,
holding the rank of Obergefreiter:
There were five concealed, well-camouflaged explosive charge sites at the power station and four camouflaged charge sites on the dam itself...
We received the order to blow up DnieproGES from Major Senftenberg (commander of the 520th Engineer Regiment battalion — editorial note). The explosion was intended to destroy and demolish DnieproGES.
“Leave nothing but scorched earth in our path of retreat” — that is exactly what we did.
🎖
