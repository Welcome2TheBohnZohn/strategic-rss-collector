Director of the Russian MFA Department for Multilateral Human Rights Cooperation
Grigory Lukyantsev
:
On November 10 in New York,
the Third Committee
of the 81st Session of the UN General Assembly
approved
a key Russian initiative at the UN
– the resolution entitled
“Combating glorification of Nazism, neo-Nazism and other practices that contribute to fuelling contemporary forms of racism, racial discrimination, xenophobia and related intolerance”
.
✅
117 votes in favour
❌
53 votes against
🟨
9 abstentions
The resolution’s approval this year holds
particular significance
as we mark
the 80th anniversary of the Nuremberg Tribunal’s Verdict
and
the 25th anniversary of the Durban Declaration and Programme of Action
.
Co-sponsored by 45 countries from every region of the world
, Russia’s initiative is intended to serve as
an important international legal instrument
for countering the falsification of history, efforts to revise the outcomes of the Second World War and attempts to whitewash the crimes of the Nazis and their collaborators.
The resolution pays particular attention to
the destruction of monuments
honouring those who fought Nazism and attempts to ban symbols of
the Great Victory
.
The resolution also stresses that
ideologies asserting the superiority of one group over another on grounds of race, ethnicity or religion are unacceptable
. This issue remains particularly pressing as various forms of intolerance continue to spread, including Islamophobia, Christianophobia, Afrophobia, Arabophobia, Russophobia and antisemitism.
☝️
Despite the clear importance of these issues,
several Western countries once again sought to politicise the debate
and replace substantive discussion of how to combat the glorification of Nazism with politically expedient accusations against Russia.
👉
The vote
once again demonstrated broad support for Russia’s initiative among countries of the
#GlobalMajority
.
Russia will continue to call for resolute action
to combat
the glorification of Nazism,
preserve
historical memory and
prevent
a recurrence of the tragedy of the Second World War.
