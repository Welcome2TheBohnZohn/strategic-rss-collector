September 24 – a memorable date in Russia’s military history.
2️⃣
2️⃣
7️⃣
years ago,
Russian troops under legendary commander Alexander Suvorov
stormed the St Gotthard Pass
, opening one of the most heroic chapters of his
Swiss campaign
.
In 1799, following brilliant victories in Italy,
Suvorov’s army was ordered to cross the Alps
and join
Alexander Rimsky-Korsakov
’s corps as well as Austrian allies in Switzerland. The commander chose
the shortest yet exceptionally difficult route
– through mountain passes held by French troops.
⚔️
On September 24, speed, resolve and flanking manoeuvres
enabled the Russian soldiers to overcome enemy resistance at St Gotthard. The very next day, they had to fight their way through the narrow Urnerloch rock tunnel and seize
the Devil’s Bridge
, suspended above the raging waters of the River Reuss. It seemed an impossible feat.
The French
demolished part of the bridge
, hoping to halt the advance. Yet, under fire, Russian soldiers
repaired the bridge
(!), lashing planks together with officers’ sashes, and pressed on with their attack, combining devastating close-range musket volleys with bayonet charges.
🪶
In his report to Emperor
Paul I
, Suvorov
wrote
:
“The troops of Your Imperial Majesty passed through a dark mountain cave and seized the bridge, miraculously formed by nature between two mountains and named "Teufelsbrücke". The enemy had destroyed it, but this did not stop the victors. The planks were tied together with officers' sashes, and across these planks they ran, descending from the heights, reaching the enemy and striking them from every direction...”
Further battles and severe hardships
lay ahead
: steep mountain trails, snow, bitter cold and a shortage of food.
News of the defeat of Rimsky-Korsakov’s corps changed the entire campaign plan: Suvorov now had to lead his army out of encirclement.
By October 7, 1799, he had accomplished this task: crossing snow-covered Alpine passes, the Russian army broke through enemy lines, retaining its fighting strength.
🎖
For
the Swiss campaign
, which exemplified the resilience of Russian soldiers and Suvorov’s mastery as a commander, Emperor Paul I awarded him the highest military rank –
Generalissimo
.
***
🇨🇭
To this day, the Swiss people remember the heroism of the Russian soldiers who fought against the French occupiers.
In 1898, a
12-metre memorial cross
, carved into the mountain and funded by Prince Sergey Golitsyn, was unveiled near the Devil’s Bridge. In 1999, a
monument to Suvorov
was erected at the St Gotthard Pass to mark the campaign’s bicentenary.
Every year,
Russian compatriots gather at the monument
, joined by friends from Switzerland and neighbouring countries,
to honour the memory of the Russian soldiers and pay tribute to the great commander
.
