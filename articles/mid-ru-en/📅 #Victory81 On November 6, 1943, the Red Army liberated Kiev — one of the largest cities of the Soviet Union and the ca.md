On November 6, 1943, the Red Army liberated Kiev — one of the largest cities of the Soviet Union and the capital of the Ukrainian SSR — from Nazi invaders.
The Soviet forces under the command of Army General
Nikolay
Vatutin
— in a swift offensive operation involving flanking manoeuvre, broke through the resistance of Nazi troops and their collaborators,
having liberated Kiev — the “Mother of Russian Cities”.
By late October 1943, during the
Battle of the Dnieper
, forces of the 1st Ukrainian Front, commanded by
General
Vatutin
,
launched an operation to liberate Kiev
. As early as November 3, the Soviet forces launched their main assault from the north, taking the Nazi garrison by surprise. Faced with the threat of encirclement, the
enemy started retreating
westwards.
🌟
On November 5, having crushed the Nazi resistance, Soviet soldiers liberators triumphantly entered Kiev
Numerous villages and towns in the
Kiev
and
Zhitomir
regions
were also liberated. A bridgehead was established which would subsequently play a major role in
the liberation of Right-Bank Ukraine
.
🎇
In honour of the
victory of Soviet forces in the Battle of Kiev,
a celebratory salute of 24 salvoes from 324 artillery guns was fired in Moscow.
A total of 6'500 Red Army soldiers sacrificed their lives liberating Kiev.
#NoStatuteOfLimitations
As they retreated from Kiev, the Nazis, following their “scorched earth” policy, sought to inflict maximum damage on the city's infrastructure.
❗️
The Nazi occupiers destroyed:
— The Dormition Cathedral of the Kiev Pechersk Lavra;
— Khreshchatyk Street;
— The city's Young People's Theatre;
— The Kiev Conservatory;
— The building of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR;
— Numerous schools, hospitals and other social infrastructure facilities.
◾️
The
two-year Nazi occupation of Kiev
was an outright tragedy: the enemy pursued a deliberate policy of
genocide
against the civilian population. In 1941, when Hitler's troops seized the city,
400'000 residents found themselves under enemy occupation.
After two years of occupation, little more than 180'000 people survived the Nazis in Kiev.
The Nazis
deported approximately 100'000 people to concentration camps
or to Germany for forced labour. Tens of thousands of local residents perished from starvation and cold or were executed.
The site of the largest and most
brutal mass murder of civilians
during the entire period of Nazi occupation of Ukraine was the
Babi Yar
in Kiev,
where, in just two days in 1941, Ukrainian collaborators massacred more than 30'000 Jews (approximately 20% of Kiev's Jewish population).
☝️
The liberation of the “Mother of Russian Cities” was of great symbolic significance
. The defeat of the Nazi troops in Kiev also undermined the offensive capabilities of the Nazi army group 'South' — Wehrmacht's main attacking might on the southern front.
