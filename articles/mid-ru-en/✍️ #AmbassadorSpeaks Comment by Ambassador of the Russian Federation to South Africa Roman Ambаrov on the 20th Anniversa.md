Comment by Ambassador of the Russian Federation to South Africa Roman Ambаrov on the 20th Anniversary of the Treaty of Friendship and Partnership between Russia and South Africa
On 5-6 September 2006,
Russian President Vladimir Putin
paid the first-ever visit to the Republic of South Africa
.
This landmark event marked a turning point in the development of Russian-South African relations and gave fresh impetus to their consistent strengthening.
🇷🇺
🇿🇦
The signing of the
Treaty of Friendship and Partnership
in Cape Town laid a solid legal foundation for long-term cooperation.
Moscow and Pretoria
demonstrated their shared commitment to systematic and strategic cooperation
, based on common approaches to key international issues, mutual respect for national interests and the principles of the sovereign equality of states.
🤝
Over the past two decades, our countries have progressed from political rapprochement to a
comprehensive, strategic and multifaceted partnership
encompassing energy, mining, science, space and human capital development. Trade and economic ties, interregional cooperation, as well as humanitarian and cultural exchanges occupy an important place on the bilateral agenda.
A significant role in advancing these areas is played by established
intergovernmental and interagency mechanisms
, including the Joint Intergovernmental Committee on Trade and Economic Cooperation, the Russia-South Africa Business Council, and joint commissions on scientific and military-technical cooperation.
A key milestone in advancing a constructive agenda in world affairs was the adoption in
2013 of the Joint Declaration on the Establishment of a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership
, which elevated relations between our countries to a new level. The document reaffirmed the parties’ commitment to developing cooperation “in the political, economic, trade, scientific, technological, cultural and other fields”, creating a foundation for further expansion of practical cooperation.
Regular contacts at the highest and senior levels continue to
demonstrate the strong momentum
of the bilateral agenda.
🌍
Of particular importance are the joint efforts of Russia and South Africa on the international fora, primarily the
#UN
,
#BRICS
and the
#G20
, where our countries advocate strengthening multilateralism and building a more just system of international relations.
People-to-people ties remain an integral part of our mutually beneficial partnership.
We are interested in further expanding educational opportunities for South African students in Russia, developing academic exchanges and strengthening cultural ties. Creative and educational initiatives contribute not only to expanding practical cooperation, but also to fostering a deeper mutual understanding between our peoples.
This anniversary is not merely an occasion to look back. It is also a point of reference for the future.
We see considerable potential for deepening cooperation in high technologies and digitalisation, as well as for further developing energy, scientific and technological and investment cooperation.
An important priority is also the expansion of direct contacts between Russia’s regions and South Africa’s provinces, creating additional opportunities for implementing joint projects.
As a more just and multipolar world order takes shape,
Russia and South Africa, united by their shared commitment to the principles of the sovereign equality of states,
can set an example of mature, responsible and mutually beneficial dialogue.
☝️
We are convinced that the spirit of the Cape Town agreements of 2006 remains a reliable compass for the continued advancement of our countries towards one another — in the interests of the well-being and prosperity of our peoples, as well as ensuring security and stability both on the African continent and on the international stage.
