# 🎙 Russian Foreign Ministry Spokeswoman Maria Zakharova’s answer to a media question

Published: 2026-11-23 17:29:57

🎙
Russian Foreign Ministry Spokeswoman Maria Zakharova’s
answer to a media question
about Norway’s restrictions on entry for Russian and Belarusian athletes
(November 20, 2026)
❓
Question: As of November 16, the Norwegian Government has restricted entry to the country for athletes from Russia and Belarus seeking to compete in sporting events. How would you comment on this?
💬
Maria Zakharova: In defiance of the decisions of the International Olympic Committee, the Court of Arbitration for Sport and international sports federations
, the Norwegian Government announced on November 13 that it intended to deny visas to Russians and Belarusians travelling to the country to take part in international competitions and to deport them if they arrived using Schengen visas issued by Norway or other countries.
The Norwegian authorities do not want to see professional athletes from Russia and Belarus,
including those competing under a neutral flag, on their territory until the “war in Ukraine” is over. They justify this measure by citing the close ties between Russian and Belarusian elite sport and government authorities.
We regard these unfriendly steps by Oslo as
blatant and outrageous discrimination against Russians on the grounds of their nationality.
This approach runs entirely counter to the universal international principle of “sport outside politics” and has nothing to do with sport in general, as it destroys the healthy competition inherent in it and turns established sporting competitions into an exclusive club for insiders.
❗️
We strongly condemn the shameful actions of the Norwegian authorities.
I would also like to point out that
Norway is far from the only country that allows itself to violate the fundamental principles of the international sports movement
, the Olympic Charter and the political neutrality of sport.
Other Nordic countries continue to take a hard line as well. For example, the Finlandia Trophy, a figure skating event in the ISU Grand Prix series, is being held in Helsinki on November 20 and 21. However, our athletes, who were initially invited by the organisers to compete, will be unable to attend because of restrictions recently imposed by the Finnish Government.
Given the exceptionally high skill level of Russian figure skaters, who would clearly have been in contention for medals,
their absence inevitably lowers the standard of the competition and devalues its results
.
We take the same view on other competitions where Western countries deny access to our talented athletes by creating artificial obstacles or directly and unlawfully refusing them entry. There is no doubt that these restrictions are driven primarily by
Russophobia
,
which has long reached epidemic proportions in the Nordic countries.
For its part, Russia
will continue to consistently defend the interests of its sports sector
and provide all necessary assistance to our national federations and teams.
☝️
We support the efforts of the Russian Ministry of Sport, the Russian Olympic Committee and the relevant sports federations to uphold our athletes’ legitimate right to compete in international tournaments, including through legal proceedings and, where necessary, by taking action against competition organisers who violate established rules.
The idea of preserving a clean, apolitical international sports movement is shared by the majority of countries, as reflected in the positions taken by many international federations.
Our country has been and remains a great sporting power, and any attempts to isolate it or push it out of international sport are futile and doomed to fail.
