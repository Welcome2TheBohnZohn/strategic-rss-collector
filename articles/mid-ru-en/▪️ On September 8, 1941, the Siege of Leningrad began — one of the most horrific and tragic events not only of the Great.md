On September 8, 1941, the
Siege of Leningrad began
— one of the most horrific and tragic events not only of the Great Patriotic War but of the entire World War II.
The siege lasted for 872 days and claimed the lives of approximately 1 million residents of the city, including more than
600'000 people
who died from starvation.
Alongside German troops, military units from European countries conquered by Hitler participated in the Siege of Leningrad —
the 'Norway', 'the Netherlands' and 'Flanders' legions,
as well as the Spanish infantry division. From the Narva direction, Baltic units —
Latvian and Estonian battalions
— were kept in reserve by the Nazis. From the north, the
Finnish army
besieged Leningrad and also shelled the city with its artillery.
The Nazis and their accomplices
planned to wipe Leningrad off the face of the Earth
and subject its population to
complete annihilation,
including by creating conditions for mass starvation, destroying food supplies, and cutting off supply routes.
***
In early September 1941, during the Leningrad Defensive Operation of the Red Army, the city was cut off by the Nazis from all land routes. On September 8, Hitler's troops captured Shlisselburg, completing the encirclement. The city was bombarded daily with artillery.
The Nazi command's orders were absolutely clear:
maintain the siege,
strictly enforce the blockade, shoot anyone crossing the front line, and bring about the total destruction of the city's population.
❗️
But Leningrad endured and never stopped fighting.
Under unbearable conditions, the city's industrial enterprises continued to operate. During the blockade, workers produced or repaired about
2'000 tanks, 1'500 aircraft, 850 warships and vessels
of various classes, as well as
225'000 submachine guns,
12'000 mortars, and 7.5 million shells and mines.
The most famous memorial to the victims of the blockade is the Piskaryovskoye Memorial Cemetery in Saint Petersburg — the largest mass military grave of World War II. Over
420'000 Leningrad residents and 70'000 defenders
of the city are buried there.

The blockade was finally lifted on
January 27, 1944,
during the Leningrad–Novgorod Offensive.

In 2022, at the request of the Prosecutor General’s Office of Russia, the Saint Petersburg City Court officially recognised the actions of the German occupying forces — along with their collaborators, including armed units formed in Belgium, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Norway, and Finland, as well as individual volunteers from Austria, Latvia, Poland, France, and Czechoslovakia —
as a war crime, a crime against humanity, and an act of
genocide
against national and ethnic groups representing the population of the Soviet Union.
