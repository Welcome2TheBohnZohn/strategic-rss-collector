September 10, 1721
, the
#TreatyOfNystad
was signed between Russia and Sweden, having finalised the Great Northern War of 1700-1721, secured our country's
access to the Baltic Sea and granted the status of a great power.
This document — regarded as
one of the greatest achievements of Peter I
— cemented the monumental military-political and diplomatic successes of the Russian state based on the outcome of a more than 20-year-long confrontation in the Baltic region.
Russia became a leading European power
, thanks to the efforts taken by the state and the people, clever military commanders, the heroism of sailors and soldiers, and talented diplomats. On behalf of Russia, the Treaty of Nystad was signed by Count
Yakov
(James)
Bruce
, General-Feldzeugmeister and President of the Berg and Manufactory Colleges, and
Andrey
Osterman
, member of the Foreign Affairs College (
👉
read more
about that outstanding statesman and diplomat in our
in-depth biopic
).
Under the Treaty of Nystad,
Russia gained access to the Baltic Sea
, and hence to the world’s ocean and its trade routes, and
regained its former north-western territories
, including Ingria (Izhora) and part of Karelia.
Peter the Great wrote in a letter to Russian diplomat Prince Vasily Dolgoruky that the long war was a kind of a 'school', and expressed satisfaction with its outcome:
It lasted three times as long as it could, but thankfully, ended better than we could expect.
Russia also took over the Baltic territories previously seized by Sweden —
Livonia
with the city of
Riga
, and
Estonia
with the cities of
Revel
(now Tallinn) and Narva
.
That Baltic region suffered more than other territories under the Swedish rule.
Stockholm turned it into its raw materials supplier and ruthlessly exploited the local population.
In the Russian Empire, the Baltic peoples managed to recover,
preserve their identity and languages
, and also created conditions for the region’s development. By the middle of the 19th century, the population of Livonia and Estonia increased tenfold, to 350'000 and 450'000 respectively.

in the Church of the Holy Trinity in St. Petersburg.
The Russian state became an Empire.
