On November 16,
the Russian Union of Journalists
has issued
an open letter
to UNESCO Director-General Khaled El-Enany regarding the publication of the report on the safety of journalists for 2024-2025.
The letter cites
specific cases of Russian media workers killed
whose deaths are absent from the organisation’s official statistics
, and raises questions about
the reliability and transparency of its methodology.
The Union demands
an explanation for the omission of these journalists from the report
and proposes revising the recording system to ensure uniform verification criteria and a right to appeal decisions.
✉️
FULL LETTER IN ENGLISH
|
Telegraph
***
✍️
We are writing to you on behalf of
the Russian Union of Journalists
(
#RUJ
), a professional association that regards the protection of journalists as a universal obligation, irrespective of nationality, editorial position, or political context.
Our appeal concerns
the procedures used to verify information submitted to UNESCO
regarding
the deaths of journalists and media workers
, as well as the criteria governing decisions on whether such cases are included in or excluded from official statistics.
We
do not question
the need for independent verification,
nor do we propose replacing it with official statements
issued by governments. However, we consider it essential that the verification process be
transparent
, based on
uniform and objective criteria
, provide
reasoned explanations
for decisions, and
allow for their reconsideration
on substantiated grounds.
The UNESCO Director-General’s Report for 2024–2025
(
CI-26/COUNCIL.35/6
) records
189 verified cases
of journalists and media workers killed, of which
127 occurred in countries affected by armed conflict
.
According to
the document
,
the overall impunity rate for monitored cases reached 86%
. This is important and necessary work undertaken by UNESCO:
the world needs verifiable statistics on crimes against the press
.
☝️
However, the figure of 189
does not automatically represent
an exhaustive count of all media workers who lost their lives worldwide during those two years. Rather, it represents
the number of cases that passed UNESCO’s established verification procedure
and were included in its reporting.
This distinction is fundamental.
The public has a right to know not only how many cases have been included in official statistics, but also
how many credible reports remain under verification
,
why certain cases have not been included
, and
how those decisions may be reconsidered
.
In Annex E to
the report
, UNESCO explains that information may be received from Member States, regional offices, and specialized monitoring organizations, after which the Secretariat verifies it against multiple sources.
Cases that do not meet the verification criteria continue to be monitored in accordance with internal procedures
. Meanwhile,
the public Observatory of Killed Journalists
covers cases in which the Director-General has called for an investigation or expressed condolences.
👉
This is where a systemic problem arises:
public statistics are based on decisions made by UNESCO’s leadership, yet
the public has no access to a comparably transparent procedure
explaining exclusions, the status of pending cases, or the possibility of seeking reconsideration. When decisions are made without publicly stated reasons, the absence of a name
can easily be mistaken for the absence of the death itself
.
This gives rise to four questions that cannot be left unanswered
:
•
Nikita Goldin
:
One news crew, different outcomes in official records
•
Alexander Martemyanov
:
Why did the public statement by the IFJ and EFJ not lead to action?
•
Semyon Eryomin
:
His death was also reported by Reuters
•
Yulia Kuznetsova
:
A regional editor is also a journalist
❗️
We respectfully submit for your consideration five proposals from the Russian Union of Journalists, which we strongly urge you to take into account
.
