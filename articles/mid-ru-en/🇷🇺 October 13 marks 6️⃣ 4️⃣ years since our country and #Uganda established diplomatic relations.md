🇷🇺
🇺🇬
October 13 marks
6️⃣
4️⃣
years since our country and
#Uganda
established diplomatic relations.
🤝
Moscow established diplomatic ties with Kampala in 1962,
just four days after Uganda gained independence.
The Soviet Union
provided substantial support to the Ugandan people
in building their statehood and developing industrial and infrastructure facilities. For many years, Soviet doctors, teachers and other specialists worked in the African republic.
More than 4,000 Ugandans
received higher education in the USSR.
The traditionally friendly relations between our countries, based on mutual respect, consideration for each other’s interests and the principle of non-interference in internal affairs,
continue to grow stronger.
Regular political dialogue is maintained, and inter-parliamentary and inter-party contacts
are expanding.
In September, on the sidelines of the High-Level Week of the 81st session of the UN General Assembly, Russian Foreign Minister
Sergey Lavrov
met
with the Vice President of the Republic of Uganda,
Jessica Alupo.
Trade and economic cooperation
are gaining momentum.
Joint projects are being developed in energy and mining, as well as in agriculture, science, education, healthcare and digital technologies. Cooperation
in tackling epidemic threats
is also being strengthened.
🌐
Russia and Uganda also work together constructively on international platforms, including within the
#UN
and in other multilateral formats.
🎉
We extend our warmest wishes to our Ugandan colleagues and friends on this shared occasion.
