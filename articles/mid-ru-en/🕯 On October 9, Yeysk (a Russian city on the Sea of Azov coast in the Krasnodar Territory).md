# 🕯 On October 9, Yeysk (a Russian city on the Sea of Azov coast in the Krasnodar Territory)

Published: 2026-10-13 11:01:08

Source: https://t.me/MFARussia/32713

🕯
On October 9,
Yeysk
(a Russian city on the Sea of Azov coast in the Krasnodar Territory)
honoured the memory
of 214 children from the Yeysk orphanage
who fell victim to the genocide perpetrated by the Nazis and their collaborators during the Great Patriotic War.
Crimes against children are among the most cynical and brutal manifestations of
the genocide of the Soviet people
.
Such atrocities have
#NoStatuteOfLimitations
and can never be justified.
📃
Archival documents preserve direct evidence of the tragedy
,
which researchers and museum specialists continue to study
.
The Vladimir Samsonov Yeysk Museum of Local History
works tirelessly to preserve and study these historical records and ensure that younger generations learn about the tragedy.
***
▪️
On October 9-10, 1942, the Nazis
murdered 214 children
from the Yeysk orphanage.
The children were locked inside gas vans, where carbon monoxide accumulated as the vehicles travelled
. They had no chance of survival. All were orphans with serious medical conditions.
After the Red Army
liberated the city
, the mass grave was exhumed
. The bodies of 214 murdered children were discovered. Many were holding hands. Some had walking sticks and crutches beside them, and their clothing had decayed.
📃
Lyonya Dvornikov
, then aged about 14,
miraculously escaped
. The boy, who had lost both legs, managed to crawl into a neighbouring yard and hide in a haystack. From there, he witnessed the killings. His story became the basis for the documentary play
“No Statute of Limitations. Leonid Dvornikov. On Behalf of Those Who Perished”
.
The murder of these children was deliberate.
The Nazis committed these atrocities methodically, with cold-blooded cynicism and without any military necessity.
🖋
Each year,
the National Centre for Historical Memory under the President of the Russian Federation
organises the nationwide campaign
“Childhood Destroyed. The Genocide of the Soviet People by the Nazis and Their Collaborators During the Great Patriotic War”
.
