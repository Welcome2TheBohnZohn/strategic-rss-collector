# ⚡️ Terms of Reference of the Working Group to Develop the Eurasian Charter of Diversity and Multipolarity in the XXI Century

Terms of Reference of the Working Group to Develop the Eurasian Charter of Diversity and Multipolarity in the XXI Century
Chairmanship: Belarus
, as one of the two original sponsors (with the Russian Federation) of the idea of a Eurasian Charter, will assume
the function of the chair
in the negotiation process to develop the Charter, with the possibility of appointing two co-chairs. Should no other Eurasian state volunteer for this function, Belarus will perform the role alone.
The initiating States and the Chair shall not be granted
any privileges or exclusive powers
in determining the content of the document.
Participation:
Negotiations will be
open to all Eurasian states
that participate in the official launch of the process.
Participation in the negotiation process
shall not affect the rights and obligations
of the participating States arising from the United Nations Charter, other international agreements and arrangements to which they are parties.
Participation in the negotiation process
shall not prejudge the positions of States
with respect to the content of the Charter and
shall not impose any obligation
to subsequently support, adopt or sign it. Each State shall
independently determine its position on the final document
and shall also be entitled to discontinue its participation in the negotiation process at any stage.
If other Eurasian states decide to join the negotiations at a later stage,
they should notify the сhair(s)
of their intent to participate. The chair(s) will then inform the signatories of the Joint Statement of the relevant requests, and participation of the applicants will be confirmed on a non-objection basis.
In order to ensure equal participation of all States in the negotiation process, States joining the negotiations at a later stage
shall be entitled to propose amendments or additions
to the text of the draft Charter, including to provisions that have already been discussed during previous rounds of negotiations.
Level of Participation:
Each participating state is free to determine
the level and composition of its delegation
. It may include experts from capitals and/or diplomats accredited in the country hosting the round.
Venues:
Negotiation rounds will
rotate among the capitals
of interested Eurasian states.
Belarus will host the first round before the end of 2026
. Participating states are encouraged to inform the chair(s) in advance of their intention to host certain rounds of negotiations.
Responsibility of Hosts:
It will be the responsibility of a hosting state to provide
necessary logistical support
for a negotiation round (e.g., meeting room, screens, etc.). Furthermore, hosting state is encouraged to provide other forms of material support to the delegations of participating states (e.g. providing meals).
Conduct of Negotiations:
The chair(s) will conduct negotiations in
an open, transparent, and inclusive manner
, aiming to achieve a consensus-based document. The negotiations and drafting sessions will be in-person. The working language will be English.
Text: The chair(s) will share
a "zero draft" text of the Charter
with participating states shortly after the launch in New York, allowing time for review prior to the first negotiation round.
Participating States shall be entitled to submit
their comments and proposals
on the text of the draft Charter to the Chair/Co-Chairs between rounds of negotiations.
Negotiation Timeline:
The negotiation process will consist of
approximately
four to five rounds
(more if necessary), each lasting up to three working days.
👉
The goal of the negotiation process is to reach agreement on the text of the Charter, if possible by October 2027, as and when it is ready.
Adoption of the Charter:
The Eurasian Charter
should be adopted by Eurasian Leaders at a Summit
, tentatively scheduled to be held in Minsk, Belarus, upon the final agreement on the document by States participating in negotiation process.
