On October 13, 1944,
#Riga
— the capital of the Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic — was liberated by from Nazi occupation.
Following a rapid advance towards the city and fierce combat on the outskirts of Riga, units of the Red Army’s 2nd and 3rd Baltic Fronts — commanded by Generals Andrey Yeremenko and Ivan Maslennikov, respectively,
victoriously entered Riga
.
🎖
A significant contribution to the defeat of the Nazi troops occupying Riga was made by
Latvian national units within the Red Army
— notably the soldiers of the 130th Latvian Rifle Corps. They fought bravely against the Nazi invaders,
helped liberate our common Motherland
and, through joint efforts, brought the Victory closer.

Riga remained under Nazi German occupation from July 1941. Over the three years of occupation, tens of
thousands of civilians were killed by the Nazis,
including effectively the entire Jewish population of Riga. The Nazi occupants plundered local resources and exploited the population to meet the needs of the Wehrmacht.
Located some 18 kilometres from Riga was the Nazi
#
Salaspils

a sinister “death factory” where 12'000 children of various ages were killed.
Thousands of them perished.
The camp claimed the lives of more than 100'000 people, including children, civilians and prisoners of war, who died from starvation, medical experiments, gas chambers and disease.

On 13 October, the people of Riga welcomed Soviet liberators
.
📄
From a report submitted to the Head of the Political Directorate of the 3rd Baltic Front, dated October 13, 1944:
The city’s population — workers, the urban poor, office employees and intellectuals — welcomed our forces with great joy and sincere affection.
The people of Riga greeted soldiers and officers with flowers, red flags and portraits of the leaders of our Party and Government.
The liberation of Riga was one of the most difficult operations
conducted by the Red Army in this sector of the front. The enemy fought fiercely for the city, turning it into a fortress. Riga — served as the administrative centre of the Nazi 'Reichskommissariat Ostland', one of the Third Reich’s key military and political hubs in Eastern Europe.
📄
From a report by the head of the Political Department of the 43rd Guards Latvian Rifle Division:
The division fought its way from the borders of the Latvian SSR to Riga, yet nowhere had the local population welcomed us more warmly than in Riga itself…
The city’s residents greeted the soldiers and officers with flowers and red flags.
#WeRemember
Around 150'000 Soviet soldiers and officers lost their lives in the liberation of Latvia.
In memory of the Red Army soldiers, a
monument to the Liberators of Riga from the German Fascist Invaders was opened in central Riga.
This iconic memorial in the capital’s Victory Park became an enduring
symbol of the triumph over Nazism in the Baltic region
, as well as a tribute to the gratitude of the republic’s residents for the heroic sacrifice of Soviet soldiers.
