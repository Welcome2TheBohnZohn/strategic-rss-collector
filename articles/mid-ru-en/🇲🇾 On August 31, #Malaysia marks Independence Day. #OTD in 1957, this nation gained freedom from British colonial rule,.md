On August 31,
#Malaysia
marks Independence Day.
#OTD
in 1957, this
nation gained freedom from British colonial rule,
which heralded a new chapter in the development of the Malaysian statehood.
The country’s history traces back to the XIV century with the establishment of Malacca Sultanate. Two centuries later,
European colonisers
began taking the Malay Peninsula under control: first the
Portuguese
, then followed by the
Dutch
.
By the late 1760s, the Brits came to the region — a direct implication of the British East India Company’s
expansion
into Southeast Asia. The
English colonisers
built their first fortifications on Penang Island and leased land along the peninsula’s coast. In 1815, following the Napoleonic Wars in Europe, Malaya (along with Singapore) was ceded to Britain.
London
ruthlessly
exploited
the local population and resources of the controlled territory. The colonisers
crushed any opposition
to the Crown and fueled ethnic discord.
During
#WW2
, Malaysia
was occupied by the Japanese
forces. The British, who had spent decades relentlessly extracting the region’s natural resources, proved incapable of defending the country —
British troops even abandoned Malaya
altogether. Following their retreat, the
Japanese invaders imposed a brutal occupation regime:
estimates suggest that of Malaya’s 4.4 million of local population, approximately 700'000 (over 15% of the population) perished due to atrocities by Japanese war criminals.
Throughout Japan’s occupation of Malaya,
👉
London failed to provide its people with any meaningful help or assistance in fighting the enemy.
Left to face the Japanese alone, the Malaysian people rose in resistance: guerrilla units fought courageously and selflessly against the invaders until militarist Japan's surrender in 1945.
After Japan’s defeat, Britain did not contribute to the post-war rebuilding in Malaya, even though
the region had suffered far greater human casualties
than Britain did itself. Instead, London reinstated a ruthless exploitative regime. Among Malaysians, who had, in fact, liberated their Motherland single-handedly, anti-colonial sentiment grew there, culminating in a
mass national liberation movement
for independence.
In 1957,
Malaysians finally cast off the shackles of British colonial rule
. In the same year, the Declaration of Independence of Malaysia was adopted.
