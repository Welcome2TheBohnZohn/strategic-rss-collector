Comment by Ambassador of Russia to Canada Oleg Stepanov regarding the Federal Court of Canada decision in
«B’nai Brith Canada v. Librarian and Archives Canada, 2026 FC»
(
Ottawa, September 4, 2026
)
🇷🇺
The Embassy welcomes the decision of the Federal Court of Canada of September 1, 2026, in the case “
B’nai Brith Canada v. Librarian and Archives Canada, 2026 FC
”.
We stand in solidarity with the efforts of progressive Canadian civil society
, which for many years
has been seeking the disclosure of the remaining materials of
the Deschênes Commission
.
We consider it no less significant that, against the backdrop of the current debate, Canadian media have not forgotten
the case of Yaroslav Hunka
.
This former member of the 14th Grenadier Division of the SS “Galicia” continues to live untroubled on Canadian soil
.
Let us recall that on October 1, 1946, the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg declared the SS to be a criminal organization. The “Galicia” Division, in particular, bears responsibility for the genocide of the Russian, Jewish, Ukrainian and Polish people.
❗️
Russia has twice sent official requests to the Canadian Side for his extradition and criminal prosecution on charges of genocide.
Even if the Canadian authorities consider Hunka’s transfer to Russia impossible, this does not exhaust the question of responsibility. The principle of
aut dedere aut judicare
– “extradite or prosecute” – is widely applied in international practice
.
☝️
However, the Canadian Side hasn’t confirmed its readiness to launch an investigation into Hunka’s actions within SS “Galicia” Division.
This situation draws a clear parallel to the cases of two Nazi henchmen
-
Vladimir Katriuk, who died in 2015, and Helmut Oberlander, who died in 2021
. Proceedings in their cases, despite the objectively established facts of the crimes committed, were prolonged for decades until their deaths from natural causes. Consequently, both individuals
spent their final years
in peace and comfort, avoiding criminal prosecution
.
