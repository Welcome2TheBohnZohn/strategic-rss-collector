Russian Foreign Ministry Spokeswoman
Maria Zakharova
’s
answer to a media question
regarding the new EU human rights report
(September 7, 2026)
❓
Question: In August, the EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy released its annual report on human rights and democracy around the world for 2025. What is your assessment of this document?
💬
Maria Zakharova:
Yes, we have seen it. And frankly,
it is a perfect compilation of all the EU’s favourite clichés and slogans:
the usual self-promotion of their supposed role as a “global champion of human rights,” a laundry list of politicised accusations against third countries, alongside intrusive and patronising lecturing.
What’s more, the level of criticism isn’t calibrated to the facts on the ground but is determined solely by how pliant each state is towards Brussels.
A case in point: the section on Ukraine, which is utterly toothless and, in places, almost fawning – this for
a country mired in corruption, lawlessness, and neo-Nazi ideology.
It is telling that
Brussels no longer even bothers to hide its use of the human rights agenda as a cover for blatant interference in the internal affairs
of states that pursue independent policies. They openly admit to funnelling significant funds into “civil society,” media, journalists, and activists in targeted countries with the explicit aim of shaping domestic politics and steering foreign policy choices.
One passage in particular stands out: how the EU frames its own policy towards Russia. In the country-specific section, they unabashedly call it a
“wartime approach”
–
as if that somehow justifies their glaring lies about our country,
their double standards, and their willingness to sweep under the rug the EU’s own violations of our citizens’ rights.
❗️
In essence,
Brussels is openly declaring that it intends to weaponise human rights as leverage against Russia.
Throughout the report, Brussels once again presumes to speak from a self-appointed moral high ground, casting itself as a “force for good in the world.” These statements look particularly cynical against the backdrop of the EU’s own
steadily deteriorating human rights record.
Intolerance is on the rise across the bloc, with racism and hate speech becoming increasingly frequent. European institutions either turn a blind eye to Russophobia, including its most grotesque manifestations – or, worse, actively condone it.
The shameful reality of mass statelessness in the Baltics
, alongside the systematic denial of education in their native language to local Russian-speaking populations, is a glaring case in point.
For all their outward talk of democracy and the rule of law, inside the EU they are clamping down on dissent through censorship, bans on inconvenient media outlets, and the silencing of political actors who dare to criticise current Brussels policies.
All of this is dressed up as “safeguarding democracy” when, in fact, what they are doing is engineering a more pliable socio-political environment, tightening their grip on the information space and on the will of their own citizens.
For a more accurate picture of the real human rights situation in Europe, including in Ukraine, I would refer you to
the joint report published in June by the Russian and Belarusian Foreign Ministries, entitled “Human Rights Situation in Certain Countries.”
It is available in English, so that anyone interested, including international audiences, can judge for themselves which document shows genuine concern for human rights.
☝️
And I think we can safely say that
the comparison will not reflect well on the opus signed by Kaja Kallas.
