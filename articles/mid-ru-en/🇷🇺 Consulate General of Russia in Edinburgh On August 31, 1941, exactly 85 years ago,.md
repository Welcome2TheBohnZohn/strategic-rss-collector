🇬🇧
Consulate General of Russia in Edinburgh
:
⚓️
On August 31, 1941, exactly 85 years ago,
the first Arctic convoy
–
codenamed “Dervish” – arrived in the Soviet Union from Britain.
It delivered
25,000 tonnes of cargo
to Arkhangelsk and marked the beginning of regular Arctic supply shipments between the USSR and the Allied powers.
During
the Second World War
,
around 1,500 vessels
, escorted by warships,
sailed to the Soviet Union and back as part of 78 Arctic convoys
. They delivered thousands of aircraft and tanks, other weapons and military equipment, as well as ammunition, medical supplies, fuel, strategically important raw materials and other supplies vital to the anti-Hitler coalition’s fight against Nazi Germany and its allies.
The men who served on the Arctic convoys were true heroes
. The voyages were carried out in extreme conditions – bitter cold, gale-force winds and heavy snowfall – under the constant threat of attack by the German navy and air force. More than 3,000 sailors serving on the Arctic convoys gave their lives for victory over Nazism.
🕯
Consul General of the Russian Federation Denis Moskalenko honoured the memory of the Arctic convoy participants
by laying a wreath at
the monument dedicated to them in Cove
, northwestern Scotland. It was in nearby Loch Ewe that convoys bound for the Soviet Union were assembled between January 1942 and December 1944.
The Consul General also visited the Arctic Convoy Museum in the village of Aultbea
, on the shores of Loch Ewe, and thanked its staff for preserving the memory of our nations’ joint struggle against Nazism during
the Second World War
.
