# 🎙 Remarks by Russia’s Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov at the High-Level Plenary Session of the Fourth Minsk International Conference on Eurasian Security

Published: 2026-11-03 14:30:18

Source: https://t.me/MFARussia/33208

🎙
Remarks
by Russia’s Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov at the High-Level Plenary Session of the Fourth Minsk International Conference on Eurasian Security
(Minsk, November 3, 2026)
Key points
:
• Honest, open discussions on ensuring reliable and lasting Eurasian security – an issue of importance to us all –
are more vital today than ever
. This is particularly true given the
#GlobalMajority
’s clear demand for
a new model of world order
.
•
Russia supports the emergence of a just system of international relations based on
#Multipolarity
. It must reflect the cultural and civilisational diversity of the modern world and its peoples, recognising each people’s right to determine their own destiny.
•
We are consistently working to advance President Vladimir Putin’s initiative to establish
a Greater Eurasian Partnership
, put forward back at
the First #RussiaASEAN Summit
, by building ties between integration associations across Eurasia. This process is already underway, involving the
#EAEU
,
#SCO
and
#ASEAN
.
• Attempts to ensure the practical implementation of the principle of equal and indivisible security
within the Euro-Atlantic
– specifically Euro-Atlantic, rather than Eurasian –
security framework have
failed
. The West proved
unwilling to cooperate
on an honest and equal footing.
•
The OSCE’s current state is frankly deplorable
. This is the result of the same confrontational logic of bloc mentality pursued by Western countries.
• Does the OSCE have a future when its history symbolises the failure of the Euro-Atlantic security model? Rooted in the illusions of the late Cold War era,
this model requires a fundamental overhaul if it is to remain viable
. Yet what we hear from Brussels are calls to build
a system of “European security” without Russia and Belarus
– or, frankly speaking,
directed against our two countries
.
•
Today, we call for indivisible security through the development of a pan-Eurasian
, rather than Euro-Atlantic,
architecture
, open to all countries and associations on the continent and reflecting the realities of a
#MultipolarWorld
.
• Commitment to the principles of indivisible security, openness and equality means that
countries harbour no hostile intentions towards one another or towards third countries
. This could involve developing
a system of mutual and cross-cutting security guarantees
at both bilateral and multilateral levels.
• We believe that the framework document –
the Eurasian Charter of Diversity and Multipolarity in the 21st Century
– should help consolidate
a continent-wide consensus
on the foundations of such cooperation.
