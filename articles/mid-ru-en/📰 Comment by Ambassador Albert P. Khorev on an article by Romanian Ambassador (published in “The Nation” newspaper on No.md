📰
Comment
by Ambassador Albert P. Khorev
on an article by Romanian Ambassador
(published in “The Nation” newspaper on November 24, 2026)
✍️
I took note of the article published by the Romanian Ambassador to Pakistan on the occasion of Romania’s Armed Forces Day. The article extols the role of the Romanian Army in liberating the country from Nazi occupation during World War II, yet
makes not a single mention of the Soviet Army, which made the decisive contribution to liberating Romania from German forces.
It would appear that this “oversight” was not an innocent mistake, but rather a deliberate attempt to rewrite history in line with current foreign policy considerations.
I do not in any way seek to diminish the contribution of Romanian citizens who, following Romania’s decision to join the anti-Hitler coalition in August 1944, took part in the fight against Nazi Germany. At the same time, I consider it my duty to remind readers that it was the Red Army that bore the brunt of the fighting to liberate Romanian territory from Nazi occupation.
❗️
Soviet forces suffered a total of 286,000 casualties during the battles for Romania’s liberation.
To overlook their sacrifice in this context is simply outrageous and hypocritical.
It is noteworthy that, unlike the current head of Romania’s diplomatic mission in Pakistan, Romania’s wartime leadership openly acknowledged the Soviet Union’s contribution to the liberation of their country.
✍️
In March 1945, King Michael I of Romania wrote:
“I turn my thoughts to the glorious Soviet soldiers who sacrificed their lives for our sacred land. Their sacrifices will forever remain indelibly etched in the memory of all Romanians and will forever strengthen the bonds of friendship between our peoples.”
To this day, Soviet soldiers who gave their lives for the freedom of the Romanian people lie buried in cemeteries in 171 localities across Romania. I have no doubt that the Ambassador is well aware of this fact.
❗️
Equally hypocritical are the Romanian diplomat’s accusations that Russia is engaged in “aggression,” particularly given Bucharest’s support for NATO’s concept of forward defence on the alliance’s eastern flank – or, put more plainly, the deliberate expansion of NATO’s military activity near Russia’s borders.
👉
I would like to remind readers that it was NATO’s eastward expansion, in violation of guarantees given to Moscow, rather than any mythical “Russian aggression,” that undermined the European security architecture.
The expansion of the alliance’s military infrastructure, the staging of large-scale anti-Russian military exercises and NATO’s persistent efforts to stoke military hysteria are the real causes of the current crisis in Europe, the repercussions of which are being felt far beyond the continent, including in South Asia.
In conclusion, I would like to emphasize that disagreements over current political issues cannot justify the distortion of historical truth.
The memory of those who gave their lives to liberate Romania must not be held hostage to political expediency.
We know that many Romanians continue to honor that memory, particularly those who reject the events that took place before August 1944.
