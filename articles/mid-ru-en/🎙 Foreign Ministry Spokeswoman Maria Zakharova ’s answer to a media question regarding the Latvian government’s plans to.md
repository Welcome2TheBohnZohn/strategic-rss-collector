# 🎙 Foreign Ministry Spokeswoman Maria Zakharova ’s answer to a media question regarding the Latvian government’s plans to adopt a “de-Russification declaration”

Published: 2026-11-27 19:29:58

Source: https://t.me/MFARussia/33708

🎙
Foreign Ministry Spokeswoman
Maria Zakharova
’s
answer
to a media question regarding the Latvian government’s plans to adopt a “de-Russification declaration”
(November 27, 2026)
❓
Question:
The Latvian authorities intend to adopt a “de-Russification declaration,” which envisages establishing a legal basis for restricting the use of the Russian language in the public space. The Latvian government also intends to toughen penalties for violations of the law on the state language. How would you comment on this initiative?
💬
Maria Zakharova:
The recent anti-Russian statements by Latvian officials once again highlight their Russophobic nature. Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs’s assertion that Riga is not ready to normalise relations with our country “even after a ceasefire in Ukraine” is
no revelation
– the entire policy pursued by that country’s authorities in recent years clearly and unequivocally points to this. Take, for example, the government’s fresh plans to deal yet another blow to the country’s Russian-speaking residents by adopting the
“de-Russification declaration.”
The Russian language has always been Riga’s sworn enemy, and
the protracted war against it is now taking on a new dimension.
The Latvian authorities are striving to restrict still further the scope for using it in the country, even though communicating in Russian is already forbidden in schools, higher education institutions and workplaces.
All Russian-speaking inhabitants of Latvia are regarded by this repressive regime as nothing more than potential “public enemies,” to say nothing of those who dare to openly express their dissatisfaction with the authorities’ policies or do not hide their “dissent” on the subject of Russia. Their prospects in today’s Latvia boil down to a simple formula:
either deportation or prison.
<...>
Latvia’s Russophobic authorities continue their
campaign against Soviet military memorials.
Justifying their shameful actions with the myth of the “Soviet occupation,” they have already demolished more than 200 Soviet military memorials <...>.
Another recent
anti-Russian outrage
by the Latvian authorities is their plan to force the dismissal of the staff of the Social Welfare Department at the Russian Embassy in Riga, which will make it impossible to pay pensions to Russian military pensioners living in Latvia – for many, their
only source of income.
<...>
Official Riga actively positions itself as one of the most zealous allies of the Kiev regime. Leading, together with Britain, the International Drone Coalition, it has already handed
over tens of thousands of UAVs
to the Ukrainian Armed Forces for carrying out regular attacks on Russian territory. <...>
NATO formations regularly hold exercises in the country, and the Alliance’s permanent military presence is expanding, creating additional threats along our western borders. The potential adversary in these exercises is named openly – manoeuvres are being rehearsed for
conducting combat operations against Russia.
<...>
Lacking the legal grounds to outright ban the transit of Russian grain across its territory, which serves
the interests of developing countries,
Riga is creating artificial obstacles to impede as far as possible the prompt delivery of Russian grain to world markets, thereby further increasing the risks to global food security. <...>.
The current Latvian authorities, of course, have
no desire for any normalisation of relations with Russia;
on the contrary, they constantly pursue a path of ever-greater escalation, even to the point of
provoking open conflict.
<...>
☝️
This is not our choice,
but we cannot ignore the threats,
and we reserve the right to take resolute retaliatory measures of a political and diplomatic nature to reliably safeguard Russia’s national interests and the rights of its citizens.
