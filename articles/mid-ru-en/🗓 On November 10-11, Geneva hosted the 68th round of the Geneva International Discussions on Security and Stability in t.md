On November 10-11, Geneva
hosted
the 68th round of the Geneva International Discussions on Security and Stability in the South Caucasus (
#GID
)
. Official representatives of the Republic of Abkhazia, Georgia, the Republic of South Ossetia, the Russian Federation and the United States of America took part. The UN, the OSCE and the EU co-chaired the talks.
Participants expressed their
shared commitment to continuing work under the GID
, which remain the only forum for rebuilding Tbilisi’s
post-conflict
relations with Sukhum and Tskhinval.
Participants highlighted the continued work of the existing channels for dialogue, including regular rounds of talks, 24-hour hotlines and meetings of
the Incident Prevention and Response Mechanism
(
#IPRM
) in Ergneti on the Georgian-South Ossetian border.
The Russian Side once again stressed
the importance of taking practical steps to ensure lasting security for Abkhazia and South Ossetia
.
☝️
Moscow and its Abkhaz and South Ossetian allies believe that priority should be given to
agreeing on legal guarantees that Tbilisi will not use force against the two sovereign republics
, beginning the process of
delimiting Georgia’s borders
with its neighbours and
resuming IPRM meetings in Gal
on the Georgian-Abkhaz border.
These measures are necessary due to
ongoing threats to the independence of Abkhazia and South Ossetia
. These risks stem both from
radical forces within Georgia
and from some of its closest foreign partners, particularly
NATO member states
that refuse to recognise the realities in the South Caucasus and continue to expand military cooperation with Tbilisi.
By joining the EU’s illegal anti-Russian sanctions,
Switzerland has effectively abandoned its declared neutrality
. With the support of Sukhum and Tskhinval, the Russian Side continued its efforts to build consensus on moving the GID meetings
from Geneva to a genuinely neutral venue acceptable to all participants.
🤝
Participants reached a preliminary agreement to hold
the next round of the GID in March 2027.
