#WW2

#Donbass
, were awarded the title of the
#HeroOfTheSovietUnion

The
#YoungGuard
was formed in the coalminers’ city of
Krasnodon
(
#Donbass
) after it had been occupied by the Nazis (July 1942). The city's residents, who found themselves in the enemy’s rear, had to mine coal — a strategic raw material for the war industry — for the needs of the Nazi troops. So, different
local underground resistance groups began to fight the enemy
in the city.
The Young Guard underground headquarters was helmed by
Ulyana
Gromova
,
Oleg Koshevoy, Lyubov Shevtsova, Ivan Zemnukhov, Sergey Tyulenin
and other boys and girls. The youngest member of the organisation —
Radiy Yurkin
— was only 14.
The selfless activists of the 'Young Guard', who embarked on the path of fighting against the Nazi occupants,
were no different from their peers.
They also went to school, engaged in extracurricular routine, dreamed and made plans for the future. But the
#GreatPatrioticWar
began, having changed radically their fate:
in the fight against the Nazis, they sacrificed the most valuable that they had — their lives.
The 'Young Guard' members started fighting the Nazis by making and
distributing counter-propaganda leaflets.
In December 1942, they set on fire one of the Nazi-occupied buildings in Krasnodon, where the enemy kept the lists of the people who were planned to be deported to Germany as slaves. About 2,500 young men and women of Krasnodon were
saved from the deportation.
▪️
In January 1943, shortly before fleeing from the advancing Red Army, the Nazi “polizei” intensified their efforts to eliminate the Komsomol-Communist underground resistance movement in Krasnodon. Using their informants, the Germans traced the young Soviet partisans and began mass hunt and arrests of the Young Guard members.
🖋
Excerpt from the testimony of Otto Schen —
chief of the Nazi 'polizei'
of during the occupation of Krasnodon:
During interrogations, all members of the organisation were severely tortured.
They were beaten till they fell unconscious; the polizei broke their legs and arms, then showered with cold water and threw into the cell. <...>
The prisoners’ bodies were covered with bruises and abrasions all over.
Neither torture nor brutal interrogation broke the spirit of the courageous youngsters who understood that the Nazis would not leave them alive.
They steadfastly and courageously endured, refusing to cooperate with the enemy. All the arrested members of the underground resistance
were executed on January 15, 16 and 31, 1943.
The deep pit of Coal Mine Number 5 in Krasnodon was where they were
brutally killed by the fascists.
In February 1943, Krasnodon was liberated by the Red Army. After the Nazis were expelled out of the city, local residents discovered the
mass burial
in Coal Mine 5 —
they saw a terrible picture:
a black abyss, blood, pieces of clothes and footgear. 71 bodies were taken out from the 53-meter depth of the mine.
🎖
Courageous Yong Guard members, who sacrificed their lives for Motherland, inspired thousands of their peers to perform heroic deeds during the Great Patriotic War.
The novel of the same name by writer
Alexander Fadeyev
is dedicated to the memory of the members of the "Young Guard" — the
book inspired many generations of Soviet and Russian schoolchildren
with its example of selfless devotion and self-sacrifice for the Motherland.
