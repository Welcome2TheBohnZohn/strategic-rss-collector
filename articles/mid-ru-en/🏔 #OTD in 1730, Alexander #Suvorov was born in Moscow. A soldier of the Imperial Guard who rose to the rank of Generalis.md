#OTD
in 1730,
Alexander #Suvorov
was born in Moscow.
A soldier of the Imperial Guard who rose to the rank of Generalissimo, he became perhaps
the most celebrated commander in Russian military history
and
one of the best-known generals in the world
.
As a boy, Alexander loved listening to his father’s stories of
Peter the Great
, whom he had served, and of
Russia’s Northern War with Sweden of 1700-1721
. Small in stature and often in poor health,
young Suvorov dreamed of joining the army
, though his family doubted he could withstand the demands of a soldier’s life.
Nonetheless, he began his career in the Guards and went on to serve in
the Seven Years’ War
,
the wars against the Ottoman Empire
,
the Polish campaigns
and
the wars with France
.
It was during
the Russo-Turkish wars
under Catherine the Great that Suvorov earned his reputation as Russia’s finest general.
His victories at
Focsani
and
Rymnik
in 1789 brought him the title of
Count Rymniksky
and
the Order of St George, First Class
. Among his honours was a diamond-studded sword bearing the inscription
“To the Victor over the Grand Vizier”
. Five years later, following the Polish campaign of 1794, he became
a field marshal
.
✍️
Suvorov was also a gifted teacher who trained a generation of senior Russian officers.
In his book
The Science of Winning
, a practical guide for officers and soldiers, he set down the lessons of a lifetime in the field. He expected his officers to exercise their own judgement and think on their feet when faced with the unexpected. For the ordinary soldier, he summed up the value of rigorous training in a few memorable words:
“
Train hard and the march will be easy”
.
Suvorov is renowned for having fought more than 60 battles without losing a single one.
His campaigns took him across what are now
at least eight European countries
, from the banks of the Rymnik in present-day Romania to the plains of northern Italy.
His final military campaign
took him across the Swiss Alps.
In September 1799, his troops crossed
the St Gotthard Pass
and fought their way through the Schöllenen Gorge at the Devil’s Bridge. After the Italian and Swiss campaigns, Paul I conferred on him the rank of
Generalissimo
. Suvorov
died in St Petersburg
the following year.
🇷🇺
🇨🇭
On September 26, 1898
, the Suvorov Cross,
one of the most striking memorials to the Russian commander
, was unveiled in Switzerland’s Schöllenen Gorge.
Carved into the sheer rock face near the Devil’s Bridge,
the 12-metre-high cross
commemorates the Russian soldiers who lost their lives crossing the Alps.
To this day, Russians living in Switzerland and their Swiss friends
gather at the foot of the cross
to honour the memory of the great commander and his men.
