85 years ago,
on November 22, 1941,
Military Motor Road No. 101
opened across the frozen waters of Lake Ladoga, connecting besieged Leningrad with the mainland and going down in history as
the legendary
Road of Life
.
By that time, Leningrad
had already been encircled by enemy forces
for nearly three months. On September 8, 1941, following the capture of Shlisselburg by Nazi troops,
the city's overland connections with the rest of the country were severed.
More than 2.5 million civilians, including 400,000 children
, as well as the troops of the Leningrad Front, found themselves trapped in the besieged city.
The city endured relentless artillery shelling and air raids.
But the most terrible ordeal was hunger.
On November 20, daily bread rations were reduced to a devastating
250 grammes
for workers and
just 125 grammes
for office workers, dependants and children.
🕯
According to various estimates
,
between 800,000 and 1 million people died of starvation in Leningrad
(more than the combined losses of the United States and Britain throughout the Second World War).
Not a single household was spared the tragedy
.*
*In 2026, April 19 was designated in Russia as
the Day of Remembrance for the Victims of the Genocide of the Soviet People
, perpetrated by the Nazis and their accomplices during the Great Patriotic War.
***
🧊
Lake Ladoga remained the only route for supplying besieged Leningrad with food and ammunition, as well as evacuating civilians.
Since September, supplies had been transported across the lake by water, but with winter approaching, even this vital lifeline was at risk of being cut off.
The solution was an ice road approximately 30 kilometres long.
It was prepared in record time, despite harsh weather conditions and the danger of vehicles breaking through the ice, which had yet to reach sufficient thickness.
On November 20,
the first horse-drawn convoy of 350 sledges
crossed Lake Ladoga, delivering 63 tonnes of flour. On the evening of November 21,
ten GAZ-AA trucks made a trial run.
🚚
On November 22, motor vehicle traffic began along the Road of Life.
The first large-scale convoy consisted of
60 legendary GAZ-AA trucks
, affectionately known as
polutorkas
(one-and-a-half-tonners), carrying life-saving supplies for the starving city.
Every journey was an act of heroism.
Drivers worked amid
bitter frosts
, constant
enemy air raids and artillery shelling
, and
attacks by enemy landing forces
.
Thousands of people
kept the road operational – drivers, military engineers, road workers, mechanics and loaders. All of them risked their lives every day to save Leningrad.
📊
During the first winter of the siege, in 1941-1942
, the ice road carried
361,000 tonnes of supplies
into the city, including 262,000 tonnes of food, while
550,000 people were evacuated.
Over the entire period of the Road of Life's operation (until March 1943), including both waterborne and ice-road transport,
more than 1.6 million tonnes of supplies were delivered to Leningrad
, while
approximately 1,376,000 people were evacuated
from the city.
The Road of Life became a symbol of the courage, selflessness and unbreakable spirit of the Soviet people.
It not only saved hundreds of thousands of lives, but also helped Leningrad withstand the darkest days of the siege.
🏅
Eternal memory to all those who gave their lives to save the besieged city. Eternal glory to the heroes of the Road of Life!
