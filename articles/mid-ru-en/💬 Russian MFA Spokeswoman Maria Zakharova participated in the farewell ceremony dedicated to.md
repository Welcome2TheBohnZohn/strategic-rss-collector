Russian MFA Spokeswoman
Maria Zakharova
participated in the farewell ceremony dedicated to
Ambassador Darchiev
that was held in Moscow. She shared a few words in memoriam:
Alexander Nikitich [Darchiev] was
a professional of the highest caliber.
He was a man who had a deep understanding of his field and a brilliant command of the tools of the diplomatic trade.
In addition, he was
a true mentor
to a vast number of diplomats. Incidentally, this is an integral quality of the diplomatic service. He introduced them to the world of diplomacy, literally helping them become not only professionals but also comrades, reliable colleagues and good people.
This is a unique combination. It’s not enough to simply be a professional; it’s not enough to simply be a good person. To serve the Motherland, to serve one’s country and one’s people, one needs a combination of these qualities — and, of course, professionalism, high moral character, and, naturally, complete dedication.
He devoted himself entirely to his beloved calling—the diplomatic service. I can say that although I did not work with him directly at a mission abroad, we worked with him constantly at the Russian Foreign Ministry. From that experience I had the impression that he truly worked twenty-four hours a day. Alas, unfortunately, he suffered from a serious, chronic illness. Yet, in all the time I knew him, he never once let this ailment get the better of him and always fulfilled both his duties and his obligations with honour and utmost professionalism. That was a true devotion and service.
Yes, physically he is no longer with us here. But I want to tell you that the memory of him — and most importantly, what he literally instilled in his colleagues and those under his guidance — will always remain with us. It is a distinct style, a distinct school of thought, a distinct approach. So, believe me,
we will never forget.
And I think you won’t forget him either. No matter how many more articles, memoirs, and monographs are published, and no matter how many generations of diplomats — who began their careers under his guidance and have now, so to speak, achieved some professional success and will continue to do so —
all will remember him fondly.
