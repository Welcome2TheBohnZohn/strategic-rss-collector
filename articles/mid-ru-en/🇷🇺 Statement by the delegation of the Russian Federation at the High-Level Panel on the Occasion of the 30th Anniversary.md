Statement by the delegation of the Russian Federation at
the High-Level Panel on the Occasion of the 30th Anniversary of
the CTBT
📍
Vienna, 23 November 2026
💬
When the CTBT was adopted in 1996 all those who were part of the process of its development and those countries acceded to the Treaty later shared a sincere common commitment to end nuclear testing worldwide. From the very beginning
the CTBT was conceived as a unique instrument
which would comprehensively ban all types of such activities.
Russia signed the Treaty in 1996 and ratified it in 2000. Since then
we spared no political effort in order to contribute to early entry into force of the CTBT
.
Unfortunately,
30 years after the Treaty is still not in force and prospects of it are very gloomy
. On the contrary we witness that some of the important countries that were the driving force behind development of the Treaty not only abandoned efforts to ratify it but openly criticize the CTBT and its verification mechanism.
Nevertheless, the commitment to end nuclear testing has definitely not disappeared. We can see this commitment first of all in those who work in the Provisional Technical Secretariat of the Preparatory Commission. Vivid example
is recent successful conduct of
Integrated Field Exercise in Namibia
which is an important milestone in developing On-Site Inspections capabilities
. These exercises provide an opportunity to test inspection procedures under realistic field conditions, strengthen operational readiness and enhance technical expertise in order to have a robust verification instrument once Treaty is in force.
Another unprecedented technical and scientific achievement is
the International Monitoring System
designed to comprise 321 monitoring stations and 16 radionuclide laboratories. Together with
the International Data Centre
it represents a unique mechanism that provides States Signatories with timely and objective information for the analysis of the events potentially relevant to compliance of the CTBT.
We believe that the main objective of all those who really care about the Treaty and a commitment to end nuclear testing should be
to preserve the architecture of the CTBT as it is
for the future when international conditions may become more conducive for its entry into force. Ideas about fixing or somehow rebalancing the components of the CTBT will definitely not bring us closer to the entry into force but rather they pose the risk of losing the valuable instruments of the Treaty.
Important role in preserving the CTBT and guarding it against destructive influence lies with
its Article XIV process
. A balanced, forward-looking and inclusive Final Declaration, reflecting the legitimate interests of all States, would make a far more meaningful contribution to our common objective. Thirty years of the CTBT have demonstrated what the international community can achieve through multilateral cooperation, scientific progress and a shared commitment to international security.
