🎙
Press conference
by President of Russia Vladimir Putin following visit to China
(Shenzhen, November 19, 2026)
Key points
:
• [During the
trilateral meeting
with the Chinese and US leaders] we talked about security matters in the broad sense of the word, about
preventing the proliferation of weapons
of mass destruction, nuclear weapons. We talked about issues
pertaining to freedom of navigation.
<...> Our discussion was held in a completely open and very candid atmosphere. It helped us
understand each other better
, and I think that
this created conditions for future arrangements
.
• We discussed some issues related to the activities of certain organisations, such as the International Criminal Court (
#ICC
), which is
clearly overstepping its mandate and making political rather than legal decisions.
• I have no doubt that
President Trump is genuinely committed to resolving conflicts
, including the conflict in Ukraine, and he is sincerely interested in
improving bilateral relations
across a range of areas, including, of course, the US-Russia relationship, which matters to us. <...> We must deal with realities as they are, and we hope that
this positive disposition will endure.
•
It wasn’t us who started the war.
We did not start any war at all. However,
hostilities had been ongoing
there for eight years: a war, in effect.
We are simply trying to bring it to an end
by providing assistance to the states that we did not recognise at first, but have now recognised, and with which we have
concluded mutual assistance treaties.
I
reminded
our colleagues of this today.
• Compromises have been reached and supported by us, including
in Anchorage.
The question is
whether the other parties to the conflict
– Ukraine and, in this case, its European sponsors –
are prepared to accept them.
In other words, do they want to reach an agreement to end hostilities, to end the war, by peaceful means or not?
Judging by what we see at the moment, they DON'T.
• As I have already said,
we are ready to make compromises
. It is essential for the other participants in the [Ukraine settlement process] to recognise
the realities on the ground
and to be prepared to make compromises on their part as well. That is all. I see nothing particularly complicated about it.
All that is needed is political will.
• [For the Kyiv regime,]
there are no incentives to agree to a cessation of hostilities
and to creating conditions for a long-term – as
I have already said
,
lasting
– peace that looks to the future.
This is precisely what we are striving for.
•
The Crimean Bridge is a purely civilian facility.
<...> We will not negotiate;
we will respond to attacks on it.
And I want everyone to be aware of that. I believe our response
will be far more painful
than what the Kyiv regime has done to the bridge.
• Working within
#APEC
is of great importance to us. We set ourselves the goal of increasing our trade turnover with this region to 40% –
it now exceeds 60%
. <...> We reaffirmed
the fundamental principles of economic relations
: freedom of economic relations and the inadmissibility of various kinds of restrictions.
• The Americans keep urging us to improve relations with Europe, but
we were not the ones who damaged those relations
.
• We recognise that Ukraine has a right to security, just as Russia does. However,
no one wants to discuss Russia’s security.
And this is a crucial point for us.
• President of China Xi Jinping and President of the US Donald Trump spoke of
China’s and the United States’ interest
, as well as that of the world as a whole, in ending the conflict in Ukraine.
Russia is also interested in ending the conflict in Ukraine.
But today, the issue is not only, or even primarily, about us –
it is about the Ukrainian side.
• Russia–China relations are one of the
cornerstones
of today’s international security system. They are an important
deterrent and a key factor in maintaining global balance.
