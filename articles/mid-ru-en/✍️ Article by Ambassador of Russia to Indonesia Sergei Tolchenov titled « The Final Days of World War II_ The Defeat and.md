Key points:
• September 3 marks the Day of Military Glory in Russia, known as the
"Day of Victory over Militarist Japan and the End of World War II"
. The defeat of Japanese militarism
eliminated the last major stronghold of the Axis powers in the Asia-Pacific region
, as well as Nazi Germany’s ally in the Far East, which had occupied vast territories and subjugated numerous peoples.
• Japan’s war in Asia and the Pacific claimed the lives of an
estimated 36 million people
and caused immense destruction and suffering. In China alone, 10–15 million people were massacred. On the island of Java,
3–4 million were killed
, while in Indochina the death toll reached 1–1.5 million. Japan lost 2.1 million military personnel and 800,000 civilians. Some 36,000 Soviet officers and soldiers lost their lives during the liberation of China and Korea from Japanese occupation.
• On August 9, 1945, the Soviet Union, f
ulfilling the commitments it had undertaken
at the Yalta Conference of the Allied powers (February 4–11, 1945), entered the war against Japan. The Red Army launched a rapid advance deep into Manchuria. By August 20, after fierce fighting, Soviet forces had reached the Manchurian Plain, split Japanese forces into separate groupings and encircled them.
Japanese troops began surrendering in large numbers.
• On September 2, 1945, aboard the American battleship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay, with representatives of the Allied powers in attendance, the Instrument of Surrender of Japan was signed, bringing to
a formal end the largest conflict in human history
, unleashed by fascism and Hitlerism in alliance with Japanese militarism.
• The memory of the horrors of World War II and its consequences
remains deeply important today
. Developments around the world show how easily some countries’ leaders, influenced by Nazi and militarist ideas and pursuing revanchist policies,
can forget the tragic chapters of their own history and their past crimes
, especially when acknowledging them runs counter to their current political interests.
