On November 5, 1957, a solemn ceremony was held in
#Plovdiv
, Bulgaria, to unveil a monument to the Soviet liberator soldier, which local residents dubbed
#Alyosha
.
This iconic memorial features an 11.5-metre-high statue of a Red Army soldier, looking eastwards. In "Alyosha"’s hand is a 'PPSh' submachine gun, its barrel pointing towards the ground — a legendary weapon of the
#GreatPatrioticWar
, used by the Red Army along their glorious path of
liberation
, first of their Motherland and then of the whole of Eastern Europe, including
Bulgaria
,
from the Nazi invaders.
The monument stands atop a six-metre granite-clad pedestal, adorned with two famous bas-reliefs:
“The Soviet Army Strikes the Enemy”
and
“The People Welcome the Soviet Soldiers”
.
🏅
For several decades, the statue has served as a
reminder of the heroism of Soviet soldiers
and officers who gave their lives to liberate Europe from fascism.
During
#WW2
, the Bulgarian government maintained its alliance with Nazi Germany for an extended period. Many
Bulgarians,
however,
refused to support this policy,
with tens of thousands of them joining the Resistance and forming one of the most powerful antifascist movements in Eastern Europe.
In September 1944, when the Red Army’s campaign to liberate Bulgaria from the German Nazis came under way, the Bulgarian army offered no resistance to Soviet units. Soon after the change of power in the country, Soviet and Bulgarian soldiers
joined hands
to fight the Nazis and
free Europe.
***
The idea of commemorating the Soviet soldiers who liberated Bulgaria was first
put forward by the residents of Plovdiv in 1948
. Following a competition involving ten creative teams, the winning design was the one submitted by
Vasil Radoslavov.
The monument was modelled on a photograph of
Alexey Ivanovich Skurlatov
, who came from the Altai region and served as a Red Army private within the 3rd Ukrainian Front and was decorated with two Orders of the Red Star. In 1944,
during the liberation of Bulgaria
, he took part in restoring the
telephone
comms
between
Sofia
and
Plovdiv
.
The “Alyosha” monument was installed in 1954
at the initiative of the Bulgarian people
and officially opened on 5 November 1957. Ever then, it has become the
most recognisable symbol of Plovdiv,
reminding residents and visitors alike of the Great Feat of our soldiers — a
heroic generation
of victors who, through immense effort and sacrifice,
eradicated
Nazism
and restored peace all over Europe.
💬

The memorial has always symbolised the boundless gratitude of the Bulgarian people to the Soviet Army, which made a special contribution to the liberation of Europe from the Nazi invaders.
