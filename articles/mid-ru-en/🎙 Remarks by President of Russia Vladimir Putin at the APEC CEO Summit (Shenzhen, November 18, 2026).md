Remarks
by President of Russia Vladimir Putin at
the APEC CEO Summit
(Shenzhen, November 18, 2026)
Ahead of the Session, the Russian President spoke briefly with Prime Minister of Malaysia Anwar Ibrahim.
💬
Vladimir Putin:
The Asia-Pacific region has become
the centre of the global economy
and
the world’s leading industrial and technological hub.
It is home to
major consumer markets
,
leading global companies
and a
dvanced centres of research and education
.
#APEC
economies account for:
•
over 60%
of global GDP;
•
36%
of the world’s population;
•
50%
of global trade in goods;
•
35%
of global trade in services;
•
over 60%
of global foreign direct investment.
The Asia-Pacific offers a clear example of how
a fairer, more democratic world order based on equality is taking shape
, as the old centralised hierarchy gives way to an extensive network of horizontal ties.
***
☝️
Experience has shown that
the old instruments of economic domination and coercion have run their course
and lost much of their power to intimidate. Yes, sanctions, tariffs and export restrictions can still slow growth, but
they can no longer bring it to a halt
, let alone crush a competitor’s economy.
The failed attempts to isolate our own country, Russia, are a case in point
. We have faced unprecedented sanctions pressure, with more punitive measures imposed on us than on any other country in the world. Yet thanks to our geographical position, the character of our peoples and the role we play in the global economy and in world affairs,
we have come through all these trials with dignity
.
Let me stress that when economies are so closely linked, artificial barriers at any point in a value chain
raise costs throughout the rest of the chain
. This is precisely what we see
in countries that actively impose sanctions on Russia
or anyone else.
📉
The EU
, for example,
has been unable to adapt to the consequences of its own measures
, including its decisions to give up access to our market and cut off the reliable supply of affordable Russian energy that underpinned its prosperity for decades. I
ts industrial capacity is shrinking
and it is losing ground to its competitors.
Budget deficits
have become a pressing concern,
debt burdens
are growing and
inflation
is rising.
All this weighs heavily on
European businesses and citizens
. Living standards in Europe continue to fall –
a humiliation for the eurozone
.
***
For Russia, the Asia-Pacific is a key region for trade and economic cooperation.
In 2014, the region accounted for over a quarter of our foreign trade, and we set ourselves the long-term target of raising that share to 40%. Today, it already accounts for
over half of Russia’s trade with the world –
53%.
We propose that APEC step up its efforts to strengthen logistics links and transport networks
to keep pace with the profound changes in the global economy and the substantial growth in the region’s export potential. Russia can offer
a wide range of transit routes by land and sea.
APEC offers ample scope for constructive dialogue at all levels
, above all among the leaders of major economies. They can sit down together
to
exchange views
,
resolve differences
between their countries on political, economic and security issues by seeking compromise and balancing interests, and
work together
to strengthen the foundations for sustainable development worldwide.
